REVIEW OF LITERATURE

As mushroom cultivation generates spent mushroom substrate as a by-product, it has been investigated as a resource for environmental remediation. The use of it as a biosorbent is a possible way of combining the re-use of the waste with the treatment of the wastewater. (Corral-Bobadilla et al., 2019) studied the removal of multiple metals from industrial water by means of SMS from the growth of Agaricus bisporus. They examined the performance of SMS operating systems under various conditions. The study is important because it examines the use of SMS in relation to the treatment of metal-containing water and not just its physical or chemical characteristics. The qualities of SMS do not need to be consistent. Variations in mushroom species and growing medium could affect the product. Thus, the properties and performance of SMS utilized in a specific experiment must be assessed instead of taken as granted from another experiment. The adsorption of Pb(II) by spent Pleurotus ostreatus and spent shiitake substrates biochar was investigated by (Wu et al., 2019).The physicochemical properties of the developed biochar and the adsorption capacity of the developed biochar’s for the removal of lead from aqueous solution were investigated. The authors found that spent P. ostreatus substrate created adsorption capacities of 326 mg/g while the spent shiitake substrate created adsorption capacities of 398 mg/g. They correlated their observations to surface area, pore structure and functional groups. The study shows the potential of mushroom derived materials for metal adsorption, but these reported values are for the specific mushroom biochar’s and conditions studied. These cannot be used as the capacity for adsorption of SMS prepared in the present study of cadmium. (et al, 2020) emerged mushrooms Pleurotus ostreatus from the spent substrate and used them for the removal of lead (Pb (II)) from aqueous solutions. The research comprised of both batch and dynamic experiments as well as characterization studies were conducted by the use of FTIR, SEM-EDX, and X-ray diffraction. Under the experimental conditions, the authors found that the spent substrate biomass can be more strongly adsorbed than the original mushroom biomass They also studied fixed-bed column and reusability of the biosorbents. The relevance of this work to the present dissertation is because it is a combination of the assessment of metal removal and characterization of materials. However, its results are for lead, and should not be compared to the results for the performance of cadmium removal. (Liu et al., 2018) studied the effect of composting on the removal of lead ions using spent mushroom substrate. The authors have compared the composted SMS with other material and have also investigated the effect of experimental parameters such as pH, contact time, biosorbent dosage, and initial metal concentration. Their findings showed that composting resulted in the change of various physicochemical properties of the material such as pH, Cation exchange capacity and hydrophilicity. The study showed that biosorption and precipitation are significant mechanisms in the removal of lead by the composted material. This discovery indicates that substrate processing may impact its function, and that removal may occur by multiple processes. The present investigation confirms these findings that it is necessary to document the preparation of SMS and investigate about the influence of experimental conditions. They do not conclude that composting will have the same changes or removal mechanism for cadmium (Corral-Bobadilla et al., 2019), studied the treatment of industrial water with the presence of chromium, lead, iron, cobalt, nickel, manganese, zinc, copper and aluminium by SMS. They found that several pollutants could be removed in the SMS bioreactors and that the performance of the treatment could be assessed under various operating conditions. The work is applicable to the general use of SMS for metal contaminated water treatment. However, the removal of one metal should not be inferred as the removal of another as the two metal ions may interact differently with the material, as well as with other constituents of the water. The FTIR and elemental analysis may provide insights into the characteristics of a biosorbent and the interaction between the dissolved metals in the biosorbent solution. FTIR can give information on the presence of functional groups and infrared active chemical bonds, and SEM-EDX can be used to investigate surface topography and elemental composition of the analysed area (Eliescu et al., 2020) used FTIR and SEM-EDX as part of the characterization of mushroom biomass and spent substrate used for lead biosorption. This gives a methodological example of how to complement biosorption results with other characterization methods. In the current study, any statement about the involvement of a specific functional group in binding cadmium should be based on the actual FTIR spectra as well as complementary analytical evidence if available. The papers found in your list fall into several overlapping, but distinct areas: adsorption of lead on spent mushroom substrate, removing lead from spent mushroom substrate using biochar, SMS for multi metal treatment, and removal of iron from SMS using mushroom substrate. All these studies indicate that mushroom wastes are relevant to the study of environmental treatment. However, their results are not compiled with each other as if they were addressing the same biosorbent, the same metal or the same experimental conditions. The current dissertation deals with the removal of cadmium with the specific SMS prepared for the experiment. Therefore, its contribution has to be evaluated based on the measured cadmium concentrations, removal efficiency, adsorption capacity and characterization results from this work.
